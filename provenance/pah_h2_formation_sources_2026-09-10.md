# Remaining PAH H2 formation: source constraints

This is source investigation within approved medium group7, not completion,
deferral, a new approval gate, or evidence that all public data are absent.

[Foley et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/1808.09192v1), sections2--3,
studies gas-phase superhydrogenated coronene cations. Abstraction from the
singly superhydrogenated C24H13+ is negligible in their adopted sequence;
the observed abstraction pathways require additional hydrogenation. Their
fit uses attachment/abstraction cross sections0.9/0.45 square Angstrom,
with the ratio better constrained than absolute values. Flux, activation
barriers and absolute cross sections are not independently determined.
Changing site topology after abstraction also changes subsequent barriers.
Consequently, simply adding their abstraction rate to the existing H0--13
model would misapply the source. Additional molecular states and a justified
site/energy closure are needed. Neutral-film measurements are not equivalent
to these gas-phase cation results.

[Barrera et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14206v2), sections3.2--3.4,
finds roughly15% of the released energy in coronene vibration in its studied
trajectories, while nascent H2 carries translation and substantial vibrational
excitation. This does not justify putting the remaining85% immediately into
thermal gas, nor transferring that partition indiscriminately to different
charge/site states. The source supplies [molecular trajectories](https://github.com/nfbarrera/Formation-of-H2-on-polycyclic-aromatic-hydrocarbons)
which remain a possible route to a declared reduced model. Reaction enthalpy,
H2 internal excitation and its radiative/collisional fate must accompany a
formation rate in the live energy transaction.

The implemented M13 H2 vacancy-refilling option remains unchanged: it
consumes molecular gas into cation vacancies and does not form H2. No new
carrier or substitute zero-rate formation path was added during this search.
